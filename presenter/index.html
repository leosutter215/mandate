What happens
  1. 0:00
    2 min
    Get them on it

    Write the address on the board. Seven laptops, each table presses its own number. There is no code and nothing to join.

  2. 0:02
    5 min
    Tables play

    Walk around. Stop at table 3 and make sure somebody actually drags the slider. That table is carrying the argument.

  3. 0:07
    4 min
    Each table reports

    Thirty seconds each, in order, 1 through 7. The bottom of their screen tells them exactly what to say, so nobody has to invent anything.

  4. 0:11
    4 min
    You close

    Their seven answers, in order, and then the line at the bottom of this page.

What each table will tell the room
  1. 1How many people is it. Say the two numbers: how many people are homeless in Philadelphia, and how many of them you would actually see on the street.
  2. 2Could the city just pay for it. Say what it would cost per year, and what fraction of the city budget that is. Then say whether that surprised you.
  3. 3The four year problem. Drag the slider to four years and say what happens. Then drag it to fifteen and say what happens. That is the whole argument.
  4. 4Nobody can see the whole thing. Say what each department can see on its own, and what happens to the number when you let them share.
  5. 5The empty lots and the closed list. Say how many empty lots the housing authority owns, how many households are on the waitlist, and what year the list stopped accepting applications.
  6. 6It has already worked somewhere. Say what Houston did, and then say what happened to Houston in 2025 when the money stopped. The second half matters more than the first.
  7. 7Three hundred and fifteen people. Walk the room down the funnel and say the last two numbers out loud: how many finished, and how many went to jail over warrants.
Close with this

Table one told you it is 5,163 people, and 711 is all you would ever see.

Table two told you covering every one of them costs one and a half percent of the city budget.

Table five told you the city already owns 1,600 empty properties, and the waiting list has been closed since 2013.

Table four told you that not one of the nine departments knows the total. Including the Mayor's office, which is the only one whose job it is.

Table seven told you what happens when you treat it as a policing problem. 315 arrested. 13 finished. 42 went to jail over warrants they could not clear.

Table six told you Houston cut it by 63 percent, and then slipped backwards the moment the money stopped.

And table three told you why.

Philadelphia does not have a knowledge problem, and it does not have a money problem. It has a four year problem.

If a teacher asks whether the model is rigged

Table 3 runs the same engine whatever the slider says, and the only thing the slider changes is how long an administration lasts. The plan is phased because the budget only frees up as earlier steps start paying for themselves, so a short term never reaches the end of the list. The step a four year term never arrives at is the charter amendment, which is the one that makes it permanent. That was not written in. It is what the model does when you shorten the term.
